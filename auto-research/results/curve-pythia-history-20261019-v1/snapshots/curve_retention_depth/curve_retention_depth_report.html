Preserving behavior during curve training—and testing what a change in depth actually means

A moderate preservation objective improves Pythia’s copying retention during coefficient-space training with little observed change in text learning. A stronger local-routing toy identifies a separate compatibility risk: even a constant parameter curve can implement a different function when sampled at a different execution depth.

96 new Pythia updates; two previous unregularized endpoints reused and checked · 19,000 tiny toy updates · five paired toy seeds · native tensor order preserved · no coefficient truncation in Pythia.

Two distinct findings. In this short run, retained behavior can be better protected while learning through full curve coefficients. Separately, exact encoding at the original depth does not establish faithful execution at a new depth. The second issue depends on the allowed state and operators, not just the smoothness or completeness of the parameter curve.

Pythia: preserve the teacher on separate replay examples

The starting point is the same pinned Pythia-70m final checkpoint and the same 24 adaptation batches used in the preceding compatibility phase. All 72 decoder tensors—18,914,304 values—are trainable. Each curve arm uses the complete orthonormal Haar pyramid of each tensor in native C order. Embeddings, final normalization and output head remain fixed. Float64 master values or coefficients feed a float32 dense network through a differentiable decoder.

The new objective is:

L = Ladaptation + λ (KLcopy + KLtext) / 2.

Both KL terms preserve the frozen starting model’s full-vocabulary predictions at temperature 1. The penalty uses 24 new synthetic copying sequences and 24 separate WikiText training blocks. Evaluation uses new copying token IDs and new validation rows. No held-out example enters the penalty. The same λ values, 1 and 4, were fixed for native and coefficient Adam before training. All other optimizer settings match the preceding phase: learning rate 2 × 10⁻⁵, β=(0.9,0.999), ε=10⁻⁸, no weight decay and fresh states.

The moderate coefficient penalty gives the clearest practical result

Relative to unregularized coefficient Adam, λ=1 raises short-copy accuracy from 44.04% to 53.46%. Text loss changes from 4.533716 to 4.536775. The paired copying gain is 9.43 percentage points, with a 95% interval of 5.39–13.59. The observed text-loss difference is small, but this was not a predeclared non-inferiority test; the interval below permits modest changes in either direction.

A next masking comparison should match update norms or retained learning and test disjoint probes; energy concentration alone is insufficient to select a safe component.

A toy that cannot bypass the depth computation

The previous truth-table task let input and output maps solve most labels without residual layers. This replacement uses a 12-site directed ring with independent scalar values. Every site must retrieve the value six successors away. Each layer can read only its own state and the immediate successor; both the input map and readout are fixed identities. Information must therefore traverse six local hops.

hi ← hi + [a(s)hi + b(s)hi+1] / N,   s = (layer + ½) / N.

Native models have six ordered (a,b) pairs, or 12 parameters. Six cosine modes represent those pairs exactly at the original six midpoints. Two modes give four parameters. All inputs, sites and layers keep their original order. The family is intentionally scalar and local, so its limitations can be analyzed exactly.

Three arms train from scratch for 600 updates using the same paired Gaussian minibatches and intermediate one-through-six-hop targets. That training-only supervision ensures a viable routing-learning baseline. Five continuation arms then start from each native teacher and train for 400 updates on only the final six-hop target. Multi-depth arms cycle N=6,12,24; fixed-depth arms use N=6.

The native model learns essentially exact six-hop retrieval in all seeds. Full six-mode conversion and two-mode conversion of the teacher retain that ability after continuation. The six-mode curve trained from scratch performs worse under the same fixed Adam rate, including two seeds near the zero-predictor MSE of 1. Its successful converted counterpart and the explicit solution below show that this scratch failure is an optimization result, not a lack of representational capacity at six steps.

Multi-depth training improves some off-grid errors slightly, while introducing six-step error, but it does not recover faithful routing across depths. The stronger task therefore distinguishes label saturation, optimization difficulties and execution-depth compatibility.

A zero-curvature curve can still lose the discrete computation

Let S be the one-site shift operator. The constant field a(s)=−6 and b(s)=6 has zero curvature and lies inside even the simplest shared-curve family. Executing it for N Euler steps gives:

TN = [I + 6(S − I)/N]N.

At N=6, every step is exactly S, so the complete computation is S⁶ and retrieves the target perfectly. At N=12, each step mixes half of the current site with half of its neighbor. Twelve such steps spread the input across many sites. More execution steps approach a continuous mixing process, not the original discrete six-shift function.

For N<6, the target lies outside the receptive field. Under independent zero-mean unit-variance inputs, any predictor in this local model has expected MSE at least 1 and chance sign accuracy of 50%. The chosen oracle performs even worse in MSE at those shallow counts because its Euler steps amplify other input values. This is a task-imposed locality bound, not a training failure.

A precise restriction of this continuous-flow family

For any real scalar fields a(s) and b(s), all generators a(s)I+b(s)S commute. Their exact continuous-depth flow is exp(AI+BS), where A and B are the respective integrals over depth. On ring Fourier mode θ, its gain magnitude is exp(A+B cos θ).

The desired S⁶ preserves magnitude on every mode. Requiring unit magnitude at θ=0 and θ=π forces A+B=0 and A−B=0, hence A=B=0. The continuous flow is then identity, which is not S⁶. Thus this real scalar, one-hop continuous-flow family cannot exactly represent the six-shift function in its continuous limit, even though the six-step discretization can.

This argument allows arbitrary depth variation in a and b; adding more scalar curve modes alone does not remove the restriction. It does not cover additional state channels, noncommuting transport, nonlocal operators, other execution rules, or a general GSA architecture.

Compatibility implication. A complete parameter curve can preserve a pretrained model at its original grid while a chosen continuous-depth interpretation changes the function class. Parameter curvature alone cannot diagnose this example’s execution error. A variable-depth design needs an explicit operator/state correspondence and a task-based error check.

The archived GSA-inspired prototype uses shared spline-generated recurrent controls and distinct memory banks; it does not specify this scalar ring operator. The result is therefore a concrete compatibility case and a design requirement to investigate, rather than an evaluation of that entire architecture.

What to test next

The moderate curve-preservation objective is now an empirical starting point for targeted updates. The next comparison should apply recursive masks or scale-dependent step controls while matching learning and update magnitude, with the full-coefficient objective as its baseline. Keep the original tensor ordering and retain all model content; masks should govern updates rather than silently deleting learned coefficients.

For depth conversion, use a state/operator construction capable of representing the intended computation across execution budgets, and compare it with the direct Euler interpretation. An augmented-state routing model or an explicit composition-preserving construction would test whether the observed restriction can be removed. These proposed experiments have not been run in this bundle.

Protocol, audit and reproduction

Data separation. Pythia adaptation reuses exactly 48 original 96-token training blocks. Replay uses 24 different training rows; text evaluation uses 24 validation rows excluded from the previous evaluation selection. Synthetic replay and test token IDs are disjoint and exclude previous copying probe IDs. All inputs were fixed with seed 2026101718 before main training. WikiText’s absence from Pythia pretraining is not established. No claim of unseen-pretraining text is made.

Replay exposure. Twelve batches of two copy sequences are repeated twice; each pair has one repeated and one scrambled query order. Teacher KL covers all query positions. Twelve batches of two text blocks are also repeated twice, with eight evenly spaced prediction positions per block. The penalty introduces extra examples and two additional differentiable forwards/backwards per update. It is not a free gain or an equal-compute comparison with λ=0.

Control reuse. The two λ=0 final decoder files were hash-verified from the preceding phase, then evaluated on the newly fixed inputs here. They used the identical adaptation stream, base model, optimizer settings and trainable tensors. The runnable source can regenerate those controls when their previous files are unavailable. No earlier evaluation scores were substituted for the new scores.

Uncertainty. Reported Pythia intervals use 4,000 paired resamples over the 24 text blocks or the 24 short copying sequences, stratified by length. Prose resamples eight passages with token weighting. They measure item variability within one short adaptation run, not variability across model/training seeds. Both λ values and all endpoints are reported; no rate search or test-selected endpoint is used. Toy comparisons retain all five paired seeds and exact operators.

Verification. The two prespecified λ=4 Pythia checkpoints were reloaded into fresh base models and reproduced every checked validation-block loss, copying metric and prose metric exactly. Expected MSE was recomputed from all 40 toy final checkpoints: 39 available final records matched exactly, and one missing final trajectory record was reconstructed from its saved model and marked accordingly. The oracle formula was independently checked against matrix powers in float64. Haar update energy passed orthonormal energy-conservation checks, and coefficient counts sum to all 18,914,304 trainable values.

Implementation notes. An initial replay cache used inference-mode index tensors, which PyTorch cannot reuse in differentiable indexing. The cache was changed to no-grad tensors before any regularized optimizer update. At the end of toy training, an audit reconstructed a matrix as float32 and failed its float64 tolerance. Audits and diagnostics were recovered from the saved models without repeating training. These fixes are recorded in implementation_notes.json. The successful Pythia run took 202.28 seconds on three CPU threads. Exact toy per-run timers were not persisted before the audit failure, so no exact toy runtime is claimed; the saved-checkpoint span was 29.17 seconds, a lower bound.
