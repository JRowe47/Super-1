SmolLM2 — Phase 5




EXPERIMENT 05 · 17 OCTOBER 2026

Joint layer curves
Optimization, constraints, and exact conversion

    
Both windows (blocks 4–7 and 12–15, zero indexed) now train together through the complete frozen remainder of SmolLM2-135M. Three matched arms test original Hermite coordinates, an orthogonal reparameterization of the same curve space, and independent dense weights for each reduced resolution.

    

Measured result.
 At three steps per window, the orthogonal curve has 1.198× original perplexity and the dense control has 1.197× on the fresh audit. Validation-gate passes: None. A deterministic five-mode curve reconstructed from the dense control matches it to within 0.000141 maximum logit difference on the 1,024-token equivalence check, while four-step outputs remain exactly native.

    

    
The useful algebraic distinction

With original weight knots fixed, a four-knot Hermite curve has four learned derivative tensors per family. At the three evaluation points used by N=3, the derivative sampling matrix has rank three: it can represent arbitrary weights at those three points. The restriction appears when the same four derivative tensors must serve all five points used by N=3 and N=2 together. That combined sampling matrix has rank four, leaving one affine constraint per weight coordinate.

    
The orthogonal arm changes coordinates, not expressivity: from the SVD of the 5×4 sampling matrix A, choose B so that AB has orthonormal columns. It begins with the exact phase-4 function. The dense control begins with precisely the same sampled weights, but can update all five samples independently. Its two resolutions have separate Parameters and optimizer state, so one cannot drift on the other's update.

    
A curve that exactly contains the dense control

We added one basis function that vanishes at all four original knots. Start with the polynomial bubble 
q(t)=∏(t−j)², j=0…3
, subtract its component in the four-mode sample space, and normalize the remainder. The resulting fifth mode spans the missing direction. The five modes form an orthogonal basis at the five tested reduced-depth sample points.

    
After dense training and validation selection, multiplying its five learned increments by this basis transpose converts them into a continuous curve with no additional optimization. The interpolation algebra reproduces the dense weights at both reduced resolutions and preserves the original model at four steps. The measured FP32 tensor/logit differences are recorded in 
checks.json
. This is a functionality construction with the same 35,400,960 trained degrees of freedom as the dense pair, not a parameter-compression result. Other sampling schedules and input-conditioned depth remain untested.

    
The four-mode projection control takes the same selected dense solution and discards its component outside the original curve space. It removes 18.46% of the learned increment energy. Its quality change measures the immediate effect of that projection on this solution; it is not a lower bound on the best retrained four-mode model.

    

    
The weight norms nearly overlap at this scale. Equal or similar norms do not imply equal weights or equal language-model behavior.

    
Matched training protocol

Each trained arm received 600 ordered minibatches of two 128-token sequences, alternating N=3 and N=2: 153,600 input tokens per arm, 76,800 per resolution. Both converted windows optimize jointly with next-token teacher KL at temperature one. The other 22 blocks, embeddings, output head and final norm remain frozen. Training draws from 1,024 phase-3 windows (131,072 input tokens). This is continuation from phase-4 weights, not training from scratch.

    
AdamW uses zero weight decay, gradient clipping, 20 warmup updates and cosine decay to 20% of peak. Training-only one-step probes calibrate each arm's average initial sampled-weight RMS update to the dense control at LR 0.0001; those probe updates are discarded. This removes a large initial step-scale difference but does not make Adam coordinate invariant. Checkpoints minimize the equal mean of N=3/N=2 KL on the first eight validation windows, checked every 100 updates. Full validation uses all 32 windows with the existing ΔNLL ≤ 0.05 and KL ≤ 0.05 gate.

    
The raw and orthogonal arms each train 28,320,768 coefficients. The dense control trains 35,400,960 across both resolutions, 25% more total, with only 21,240,576 active at N=3 or 14,160,384 at N=2. Data exposure and updates are matched; parameter storage and wall time are not. Dense training overlaps the curve worker on a separate four-thread process. Inference timing runs after all training workers finish. Comparing phase 4 with phase 5 combines joint training, more data, a different objective and more updates; it does not isolate the causal effect of joint training alone.

    
Fresh document-held-out audit

We reserved two nonoverlapping 257-token spans from each of 32 articles unused in phase-2 and phase-3 training; phase 4 used a subset of phase 3. These articles come from the official WikiText-2 
train
 partition and are held out at document level for this experiment. This is not the official benchmark test split and does not establish absence from the reference model's pretraining. No audit result selected weights, depths, learning rates or basis functions. Main scoring uses the last 129 tokens of each span: 8,192 predicted tokens at context 128.

Negative values indicate compensation relative to the additive loss prediction; positive values indicate amplification. These counterfactual ablations probe the fitted model, not a separate randomized test of joint versus independent training.

Export, caches and cost

Four-step curves preserve native logits exactly. DynamicCache correctness, dense-to-curve reconstruction, checkpoint reloads and frozen-original hashes are checked. A fixed-resolution native export samples the weights once and folds each residual multiplier into the attention output and MLP down-projection matrices. Its forward pass contains ordinary decoder blocks and no curve sampling. This export gives up runtime resolution changes until recompiled. The public five-mode loader was also exercised through an ordinary safetensors save and Transformers reload: the 28-block model reproduced all checked logits exactly (32 input tokens).

What this supports

The difference between original and orthogonal coordinates measures optimization sensitivity within the same curve space. The dense projection measures a concrete restriction of the four-mode basis. The five-mode lift demonstrates that a continuous representation can exactly carry the fitted reduced-depth transformations at the tested resolutions while retaining the original knots. None of those facts by itself makes the reduced-depth model equivalent to the original 30-block model.

    
The close curve/dense results make recovery of the reduced-depth model the next priority. A useful next comparison is retained-native-block initialization versus the present interpolated initialization, with a larger distillation budget. Any better dense variants can then be lifted into the complete curve without adding a representation error at the fitted resolutions. A separate set of depth sample points is needed to test interpolation beyond those resolutions; this experiment does not establish that generalization. This experiment does not implement the proposed GSA input conditioning, hierarchical state, expert bundling or from-scratch training.
